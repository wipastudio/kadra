4. Playing with other people

Kadra is a game about showing your own photos to people you invited. You are responsible for what you put into a game.

There is no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive users. You accept these rules before you can play, on a screen shown the first time the app opens.

  • Only share what you are comfortable sharing. You choose which photos can enter the game. If one of yours comes up that you do not want shown, the Shield (three per night) pulls it off the table mid-reveal and it never comes back.
  • Do not share photos of other people without their consent, and do not share content that is unlawful, sexually explicit, hateful, violent, harassing, or that depicts a minor in any sexual or exploitative way.
  • Do not harass, threaten or bully other players.

How we keep objectionable content out

  • Filtering. When iOS's Sensitive Content Warning is on for the device, photos are screened there with Apple's sensitive content analysis before they can enter a round, including photos sent in Jury. A photo that is flagged is dropped from the game and replaced, and in room play it is not sent to the other players at all. No image leaves your device to be analysed. Where that iOS setting is off, the Shield and the controls below are what protect the table.
  • Flagging. Any player can report a photo or a player from three places: the flag on the photo itself during a round, the player list during a game (the people button at the top of the game screen), and the results screen at the end of the night.
  • Blocking. Any player can block another player from the player list during a game and from the results screen. Blocking takes effect at once: that player's photo leaves the table immediately, their part in the round is voided, and they cannot join your rooms again. If you are the host, blocking also removes them from the room. Every block is also reported to us, with the blocked player's random in-app ID and nothing else.
  • One block is a warning; several blocks remove a player. Being blocked by one player is not a ban: the blocked player can still open and join other rooms, and the app tells them that someone blocked them and asks them to take more care. A player blocked by two or more different players (a report counts as a block) is removed automatically: their device can no longer open or join rooms with other phones, and the app tells them so. Unblocking someone withdraws your block. Blocks count for 180 days.
  • Reports reach us, with the photo. A report is sent to Wipa Studios with the reason you picked, anything you wrote, the reported player's random in-app ID, and the single photo that was on screen — so that we can actually judge it and remove it. No player name is sent. The photo is not attached if it does not belong to the player being reported, if its owner had already withdrawn it with the Shield, or if the round was void. It is deleted after 30 days, and no app client can read or list the store it goes to. The report screen lists exactly what is sent before you send it.
  • We act within 24 hours. We respond to reports of objectionable content within 24 hours of receiving them: we remove the content and eject the user who provided it — their device can no longer open or join rooms with other phones.

Game photos are relayed between players and not kept by us, so we cannot look at a photo after a game ends — which is why the report you send, and the steps above, are what we act on. If someone behaves in a way that needs our attention, you can also email wipastudios@gmail.com with what happened. We will respond, and we may refuse service to devices involved in abuse.
