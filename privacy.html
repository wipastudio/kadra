Kadra

Privacy Policy

Kadra is a game made of your own photos, so this is the part that matters most. It is written to be checkable, not reassuring.

Last updated: 22 September 2026 (reports are sent to us) · applies to Kadra 1.0 for iOS

The short version. There are no accounts. Your photos are never stored on any server, and no image is ever sent to analytics. Nothing you do in the app is tied to your name, your email or your Apple Account — only to a random identifier created on your device.

1. Who we are

Kadra is developed by Wipa Studios ("we", "us"). For anything in this policy, write to wipastudios@gmail.com.

2. Your photos

You choose what enters the game

When a night starts you choose the scope: your whole library, specific albums, or photos picked one by one. Kadra reads photos through Apple's PhotoKit and only within the scope you picked. If you grant access to selected photos only, Kadra can see nothing else.

What is excluded automatically

  • Screenshots and document scans.
  • Anything created in the last 48 hours.
  • Your Hidden album — iOS does not expose it to apps, so Kadra never sees it.
  • Photos you marked "never show this one again".

Each candidate is additionally checked with Apple's on-device Sensitive Content Analysis. That analysis runs on your device; its result never leaves it.

Playing in the same room

Devices find each other over the local network using Apple's MultipeerConnectivity, and the hand's photo is sent directly from one device to the others. Nothing goes to the internet, and nothing goes to us.

Playing apart

The photo for the current hand passes through a realtime message channel operated by Supabase so the other players can see it. It is relayed, not stored: it is not written to a database or to file storage, and it is not retained after the hand. We cannot retrieve a photo after the fact, because there is nowhere for it to have been kept.

What is never transmitted

Regardless of mode, the following never leave your device: the photo file name, its local identifier, the location where it was taken, its album name, the result of the sensitive content analysis, and which photo you removed with the shield.

3. Usage analytics

Kadra records which screens you open and which buttons you press, so we can decide what to improve. These events are sent to Google Firebase Analytics, which processes them on our behalf. You can switch this off at any time: Settings → Your data → Share anonymous usage. Turning it off stops collection entirely.

What an event contains

The name of a screen or button, the game mode being played, how many people are in the room, whether this device opened the room or joined it, whether a photo finished loading and how long it took, and the size band of a transferred photo. That last pair is how we tell whether the app is actually working for people — an empty frame is a bug we cannot otherwise see.

What an event never contains

By design, and enforced by an automated check that runs on every build: any photo or thumbnail, a file name or identifier, a capture location, an album name, the text of a report, your nickname, or a room code. No image, and nothing derived from an image, is ever sent to analytics.

The identifier, in plain terms

Events are grouped under a random identifier generated on your device when you first open the app. It exists so that, when something breaks, we can follow one sequence of steps and see where it broke. We want to be exact about what this does and does not mean:

  • It is not derived from your device. We do not use the IDFA or the vendor identifier.
  • It is not linked to your name, your email, your Apple Account or your purchases, because Kadra has none of those.
  • It does connect your own events to each other. That makes it pseudonymous rather than anonymous, and we would rather say so than use a word that sounds better than it is.
  • It cannot be reset from inside the app. Earlier versions had a reset button; it was removed when gift nights (below) were added, because a gift is tied to this identifier and a resettable one would make a one-night gift reusable. The identifier lives only in the app's own storage: deleting the app discards it, and a fresh install gets a new one.
  • Gift nights. We can grant a single premium night by hand to a specific installation or to a specific room code. When you host a room without a premium pass, the app sends this identifier and the room code to our server and asks whether such a gift exists. The answer is yes or no. If it is no, nothing is stored. If it is yes, the server records which installation used the gift and when, so it cannot be used twice.

Firebase also assigns its own installation identifier and records approximate region, device model and app version. Google's terms for this service are at firebase.google.com/terms.

4. Sample frames — off unless you turn it on

This is the only place where a photo of yours can leave your device for us, and it happens only if you choose it. During setup you are asked a direct question with two equal answers and neither is pre-selected; you cannot skip past it without answering. The default state is off. You can change the answer at any time in Settings → Your data.

What is sent, if you say yes

  • One frame per night, at most, chosen at random from the hands you play — not the first one, so that it is not always the photo people open with.
  • Only your own photo. A frame belonging to another player in the room is never sent: they did not give this permission, and it is not ours to give on their behalf.
  • Never a frame you removed with the shield. The shield decision is checked before anything is prepared for sending.
  • The image is the same 800-pixel re-encoded JPEG the game itself uses. Because it is drawn from a rendered frame rather than copied from the original file, EXIF metadata, including GPS coordinates, does not survive that step.
  • Alongside it: the identifier described above, a timestamp, and the app version. Nothing else — no file name, no local identifier, no capture location, no album name, no nickname, no room code.

What we do with it

Two things, and we would rather name both than describe only the more comfortable one. First, to verify that frames are being decoded, resized and displayed correctly across devices — an unreadable image is a bug we cannot see from numbers alone. Second, to understand what kind of material the game is actually working with, which informs how the pool filters and the modes are designed.

How long, and how to remove it

Frames are deleted automatically 30 days after they are sent; this is enforced by a storage lifecycle rule, not by anyone remembering to do it. You can delete everything you have sent immediately from Settings → Your data → Frames you have sent → Delete. That button works whether or not the setting is currently on, and “Delete everything” removes them too. Stored frames are readable only by us; the storage rules deny read access to every client.

If you say no

Nothing is sent, and the game is identical in every other respect. There is no feature, mode or content behind this answer.

5. Purchases

Kadra Premium is sold through Apple's In-App Purchase. We never see your payment details; Apple handles the transaction.

To know whether your purchase is active, we use RevenueCat, Inc. as a subscription management service. RevenueCat receives your purchase history and an anonymous identifier generated for your installation. It does not receive your name, your email or your Apple Account, and we do not link that identifier to any identity. RevenueCat's own privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

The check does not rest on your device alone. So that a paid feature cannot be unlocked by editing the app, the app sends the random identifier described above and the anonymous identifier RevenueCat generated for this installation to our server. The server asks RevenueCat whether that installation has an active purchase and stores the answer: premium yes or no, whether it came from a purchase or a gift night, until when, and the time of the check. Your payment details are not part of this and never reach us. If our server cannot answer, the app falls back to what the store SDK reports, so a purchase still works when the network does not.

6. Keeping the service usable

Anyone can send requests to our server, so each request is counted against two keys: the installation identifier above, and a one-way hash of the IP address the request arrived from. The address itself is never written down — only the hash, which is salted with a key that never leaves the server, so it cannot be turned back into an address. Counters hold nothing else: no request content, no photo, no room code. They are deleted after two days by a scheduled job.

8. Reports and blocks

If you block a player, that decision stays on your device: they are removed from the table at once and cannot join your rooms again. Nothing about a block is sent anywhere.

If you report a photo or a player, the report is stored on your device, the player is blocked for you, and a copy of the report is sent to us: the reason you picked, anything you wrote, and the same six fields listed in section 9 (app version and build, language, device model, install identifier).

The photo that was on screen is sent with it, and only that one photo — never the rest of the night, and never a photo from any other round. It is not sent at all if the photo does not belong to the player you are reporting, if its owner had already withdrawn it with the Shield, or if the round was void; in those cases the report goes without an image and says so. The reported player's name is never sent — we hold no identity for them, and it would not help us. The report screen lists exactly what is sent before you send it.

The photo goes to a private store that no app client can read, list or write to — every upload passes through a server function that checks the file and generates the path. We delete it after 30 days.

This changed in September 2026, and it is a change in the other direction from the rest of this policy, so we will say plainly why: the App Store requires that reporting and blocking in an app with user content actually notify the developer, so that objectionable content can be removed within 24 hours. A report that never left your phone could not do that, and neither could one that described a photo we were never allowed to see. This is the one place where a photo leaves a device without its owner's consent, and the reason is safety, not convenience.

9. Feedback you send us

Settings has a feedback box. Nothing is sent from it unless you write something and tap Send — there is no background collection here.

When you do send, we receive your message together with the app version and build, your language setting, your device model (for example iPhone17,1) and your install identifier from section 3. No photo is ever attached to a feedback message, and nothing else from the app is included. (Reports are the separate case, with their own screen and their own warning — section 8.) The screen lists these fields, with their real values, before you send.

Feedback and reports (section 8) are the only places where something you write leaves your device, and both are deliberate: you type it, you can read exactly what goes with it, and it only moves when you tap the button.

We use feedback only to understand and fix problems. Messages are deleted after 180 days. To have yours deleted sooner, email us with your install identifier (Settings → Your data → copy) and we will remove it. Feedback is limited to five messages per install per day.

10. Children

Kadra is not directed at children. Because the game is played with whatever photos are on the players' phones, its content is determined by the people at the table, and the App Store age rating reflects that. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that concerns you, write to us.

11. Your rights

Because there are no accounts, we hold no personal data about you that we could look up, export or delete on request — there is no record with your name on it. The data the app creates lives on your device and you can erase it from Settings → Your data; the one thing that stays is the install identifier from section 3, which deleting the app removes. The exceptions are feedback you chose to send (section 9) and reports you chose to send (section 8): those messages sit on our server under that identifier, so those we can find and delete — email us with the identifier and we will. For purchase records held by Apple or RevenueCat, contact wipastudios@gmail.com and we will help.
