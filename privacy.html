Privacy Policy

Kadra is a game made of your own photos, so this is the part that matters most. It is written to be checkable, not reassuring.

The short version. There are no accounts. Game photos go only to the phones at your table and are not kept on any server. There are exactly two exceptions, and the app shows each one before it happens: a photo someone reports reaches us and is deleted after 30 days, and sample frames reach us only if you switch them on. No image is ever sent to analytics. Nothing you do in the app is tied to your name, your email or your Apple Account — only to random identifiers created on your device.

1. Who we are

Kadra is developed by Wipa Studios ("we", "us"), which decides how the data described here is used. For anything in this policy, write to wipastudios@gmail.com.

2. Your photos

You choose what enters the game

When a night starts you choose the scope: your whole library, specific albums, or photos picked one by one. Kadra reads photos through Apple's PhotoKit and only within the scope you picked. If you grant access to selected photos only, Kadra can see nothing else.

What is excluded automatically

When the scope is your whole library or an album:

  • Screenshots and document scans.
  • Anything created in the last 48 hours.

Photos you pick one by one are used exactly as you picked them; those two filters do not apply to them. In every scope:

  • Your Hidden album — iOS does not expose it to apps, so Kadra never sees it.
  • Photos you marked "never show this one again".

When iOS's Sensitive Content Warning is switched on for your device (Settings → Privacy & Security), each photo is also checked with Apple's on-device Sensitive Content Analysis before it can enter a hand, including photos you send in Jury. A photo it flags is dropped without being shown or sent. That analysis runs on your device; its result never leaves it. When the iOS setting is off, iOS does not run the check, and the Shield, reporting and blocking are what protect the table — Settings → Your data shows which applies on your device.

Playing in the same room

Devices find each other over the local network using Apple's MultipeerConnectivity, and the hand's photo is sent directly from one device to the others. Nothing goes to the internet, and nothing goes to us.

Playing apart

The photo for the current hand passes through a realtime message channel operated by Supabase so the other players can see it. It is end-to-end encrypted with keys that exist only on the players' devices, so the relay cannot read it. It is relayed, not stored: it is not written to a database or to file storage, and it is not retained after the hand.

What goes with a photo

The other players receive the image itself (re-encoded at 800 pixels, without its original metadata) and, in modes that ask when a photo was taken, its capture date.

What is never transmitted

Regardless of mode, the following never leave your device: the photo file name, its local identifier, the location where it was taken, its album name, the result of the sensitive content analysis, and which photo you removed with the shield.

3. Usage analytics

Kadra records which screens you open and which buttons you press, so we can decide what to improve. These events are sent to Google Firebase Analytics, which processes them on our behalf. Analytics is on when you install the app, and you can switch it off at any time: Settings → Your data → Share anonymous usage. Turning it off stops collection entirely. (The switch says "anonymous" because it is short; below is the exact meaning.)

What an event contains

The name of a screen or button, the game mode being played, how many people are in the room, whether this device opened the room or joined it, whether a photo finished loading and how long it took, the size band of a transferred photo, whether photo access was granted, the type of network connection, purchase events (which product, and whether the purchase completed), and that a report or a block happened (for a report, only the reason category you picked).

What an event never contains

By design, and enforced by an automated check that runs on every build: any photo or thumbnail, a file name or identifier, a capture location, an album name, the text of a report, your nickname, a player's ID, or a room code. No image, and nothing derived from an image, is ever sent to analytics.

The identifiers, in plain terms

Kadra creates a random install identifier on your device when you first open the app. It exists so that, when something breaks, we can follow one sequence of steps and see where it broke, and so our server can apply limits and keep your purchase status. We want to be exact about what this does and does not mean:

  • It is not derived from your device. We do not use the IDFA, and the app tells Firebase not to collect the vendor identifier (IDFV).
  • It is not linked to your name, your email or your Apple Account, because Kadra has none of those. It is linked on our server to whether you have Premium (section 5).
  • It does connect your own events to each other. That makes it pseudonymous rather than anonymous, and we would rather say so than use a word that sounds better than it is.
  • It cannot be reset from inside the app. A gift (below) is tied to this identifier, and a resettable one would make a one-night gift reusable. It lives only in the app's own storage: deleting the app discards it, and a fresh install gets a new one.
  • Gift nights. We can grant a single premium night by hand to a specific installation or to a specific room code. When you host a room without Premium, the app sends this identifier and the room code to our server and asks whether such a gift exists. The answer is yes or no. If it is no, nothing is stored. If it is yes, the server records which installation used the gift and when, so it cannot be used twice; that record is kept for as long as the gift exists.

Firebase also assigns its own app instance identifier and records approximate region (derived from the IP address, which Google does not store for us), device model and app version. Google's terms for this service are at firebase.google.com/terms.

Separately, Kadra has a player ID: a second random identifier that the other players in your room see, the way they see your nickname. Section 8 explains what it is for.

4. Sample frames — off unless you turn it on

This is the only place where a photo of yours can leave your device for us by your own choice. It is off by default, the app never switches it on for you, and nothing in the game depends on it. You can turn it on or off at any time in Settings → Your data → Send a sample frame.

What is sent, if you turn it on

  • One frame per night, at most, chosen at random from the hands you play — not the first one, so that it is not always the photo people open with.
  • Only your own photo. A frame belonging to another player in the room is never sent: they did not give this permission, and it is not ours to give on their behalf.
  • Never a frame you removed with the shield. The shield decision is checked before anything is prepared for sending.
  • The image is the same 800-pixel re-encoded JPEG the game itself uses. Because it is drawn from a rendered frame rather than copied from the original file, EXIF metadata, including GPS coordinates, does not survive that step.
  • Alongside it: the install identifier described above and the time it arrived. Nothing else — no file name, no local identifier, no capture location, no album name, no nickname, no room code.

What we do with it

Two things, and we would rather name both than describe only the more comfortable one. First, to verify that frames are being decoded, resized and displayed correctly across devices — an unreadable image is a bug we cannot see from numbers alone. Second, to understand what kind of material the game is actually working with, which informs how the pool filters and the modes are designed.

How long, and how to remove it

Frames are deleted automatically 30 days after they are sent, by a scheduled job on our server. You can delete everything you have sent immediately from Settings → Your data → Frames you have sent → Delete. That button works whether or not the setting is currently on, and “Delete everything” removes them too. Stored frames are readable only by us; the storage rules deny read access to every client.

5. Purchases

Kadra Premium is sold through Apple's In-App Purchase. We never see your payment details; Apple handles the transaction.

To know whether your purchase is active, we use RevenueCat, Inc. as a subscription management service. RevenueCat receives your purchase history, an anonymous identifier it generates for your installation, and the Firebase app instance identifier from section 3, so that a purchase can be matched to the screen it was made from. It does not receive your name, your email or your Apple Account. RevenueCat's own privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

The check does not rest on your device alone. So that a paid feature cannot be unlocked by editing the app, the app sends the install identifier and RevenueCat's identifier for this installation to our server. The server asks RevenueCat whether that installation has an active purchase and stores the answer: premium yes or no, whether it came from a purchase or a gift night, until when, and the time of the check. That record is deleted 180 days after the last check. Your payment details are not part of this and never reach us. If our server cannot answer, the app falls back to what the store SDK reports, so a purchase still works when the network does not.

6. Keeping the service usable

Anyone can send requests to our server, so each request is counted against two keys: the installation identifier above, and a one-way hash of the IP address the request arrived from. The address itself is never written down — only the hash, which is salted with a key that never leaves the server, so it cannot be turned back into an address. Counters hold nothing else: no request content, no photo, no room code. They are deleted after two days by a scheduled job.

8. Reports and blocks

A block or a report is attached to a player ID, not to a nickname — nicknames are not unique, and blocking one would otherwise catch an innocent player with the same name. Each device generates its player ID once, at random, and keeps it. It means nothing outside Kadra, and it is shared with the people in your room exactly the way your nickname is. Without a lasting ID a block would last one evening: the blocked player would come back the next night under a new number. “Delete everything” resets the ID too, which makes your device a new player.

Blocking

If you block a player, they are removed from your table at once and cannot join your rooms again; the block is kept on your device. We are told too: the app sends us the blocked player's ID and nothing else — no name, no photo, no note. App Store rules require that blocking notify the developer, and the block screen says so before you confirm.

On our server, each block is counted as a pair: the blocked player's ID and the install identifier of the device that blocked them (section 3), so that one device blocking the same player twice counts once. Unblocking someone sends the same ID with "unblock" and deletes your pair. One block is not a ban: the app on the blocked player's phone asks our server, using only its own player ID, how many devices have blocked it, and shows a warning when the number goes up. It is never told who. At two or more blocking devices (a report counts as a block) the player is removed from rooms automatically, as described below.

Reporting

If you report a photo or a player, the report is stored on your device, the player is blocked for you, and a copy of the report is sent to us: the reason you picked, anything you wrote, the reported player's ID, and the same fields listed in section 9 (app version and build, language, device model, install identifier).

The photo that was on screen is sent with it, and only that one photo — never the rest of the night, and never a photo from any other round. It is not sent at all if the photo does not belong to the player you are reporting, if its owner had already withdrawn it with the Shield, or if the round was void; in those cases the report goes without an image and says so. The reported player's name is never sent. The report screen lists exactly what is sent before you send it.

The photo goes to a private store that no app client can read, list or write to — every upload passes through a server function that checks the file and generates the path. If a report or a block cannot be sent because you are offline, it waits on your device and is sent the next time the app opens.

What we do with a report, and when a player is removed

We review reports within 24 hours. Content that breaks the Terms of Use is deleted, and the player ID that brought it is added to a list of removed players: a device with that ID can no longer open or join rooms with other phones. A player ID blocked from two or more devices is removed the same way, automatically, for as long as those blocks stand. That list holds the player ID, the date and our note, nothing else, and it is kept for as long as the removal stands. Removal is tied to the player ID, so it does not follow a person to a fresh install — without accounts nothing can, and we would rather say so.

This is the one place where a photo can leave a device without its owner's consent, and the reason is safety, not convenience: the App Store requires that objectionable content in an app with user content can actually be removed by the developer.

9. Feedback you send us

Settings has a feedback box. Nothing is sent from it unless you write something and tap Send — there is no background collection here.

When you do send, we receive your message together with the app version and build, your language setting, your device model (for example iPhone17,1) and your install identifier from section 3. No photo is ever attached to a feedback message, and nothing else from the app is included. (Reports are the separate case, with their own screen and their own warning — section 8.) The screen lists these fields, with their real values, before you send.

Feedback and reports (section 8) are the only places where something you write leaves your device, and both are deliberate: you type it, you can read exactly what goes with it, and it only moves when you tap the button.

We use feedback only to understand and fix problems. Feedback is limited to five messages per install per day; reports and blocks have their own, higher limit, so writing to us never uses up your ability to report.

11. Children

Kadra is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. Because the game is played with whatever photos are on the players' phones, its content is determined by the people at the table. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that concerns you, write to us and we will delete what we hold for that installation.

12. Your rights

There are no accounts, but the identifiers above are still personal data, and you have the right to see, correct, export or delete what is held under them, and to object to or withdraw consent for any of it. Most of it you can do yourself: switch analytics or sample frames off, delete frames you have sent, or erase everything on the device from Settings → Your data. For data on our server (feedback, reports, premium status, sample frames) and for analytics data held by Google, email wipastudios@gmail.com with your install identifier (Settings → Your data → copy) and we will act on it within 30 days. For purchase records held by Apple or RevenueCat, write to us and we will help.

We use the data in this policy because it is needed to run the game you asked for (rooms, purchases, reports), because we have a legitimate interest in keeping the app working and safe (analytics, request counters, the removed-player list), or because you chose it (sample frames, feedback). If you are in the EU, the UK or a similar jurisdiction and think we have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to your data-protection authority; we would like to hear from you first.

13. Changes

If this policy changes materially, the "last updated" date above changes and the new version is published here before the app version it describes is released.
